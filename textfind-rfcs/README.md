# 📌 Executive Summary

Enterprise software has continuously evolved by introducing new
architectural abstractions.

-   Data Processing Platforms organized data.
-   Workflow Engines organized work.
-   AI Agent Frameworks organized reasoning.

This research explores the emergence of another architectural
abstraction:

> **Governed Execution**

As AI systems become increasingly autonomous, organizations require
execution to become policy-aware, trustworthy, accountable, and
attributable.

The central premise is:

> **Intelligence creates potential.**

> **Execution realizes value.**

> **Governance enables trusted execution.**

> **Attribution makes contribution visible.**

> **Economic participation enables ecosystems to grow around shared
> execution infrastructure.**

This repository explores the architectural models required to support
that evolution, including independently operated Deployment Units,
distributed value creation, attributable contribution, and economic
participation by operators, capability developers, integrators, domain
specialists, educators, adoption partners, and other ecosystem
participants.

# 🧭 Domain Capabilities & Portable Governed Execution

**TF-RFC-0020 --- Domain Capabilities and Portable Governed Execution**
introduces the **Domain Capability** as a higher-level architectural
abstraction for coherent abilities that operate through governed
execution.

The RFC extends the portability discussion beyond software and
workloads:

> **Define Once. Execute Governably Anywhere.**

Portable Governed Execution asks whether a capability can execute across
compatible environments **without losing the authority, policy,
provenance, accountability, constraints, and evidence properties that
make its execution trustworthy**.

The model deliberately generalizes beyond traditional Business
Capabilities. Domain Capabilities may belong to enterprise operations,
robotics, manufacturing, healthcare, scientific systems, infrastructure,
transportation, or other domains.

The architectural progression becomes:

``` text
Domain
   ↓
Domain Capability
   ↓
Governed Execution
   ↓
Compatible Logical Execution Environment
   ↓
Infrastructure
```

This creates a separation between **what a system is capable of doing**,
**how its execution is governed**, and **where that execution is
physically realized**.

TF-RFC-0020 is intentionally implementation-independent. It publicly
establishes the architectural direction while leaving implementation
mechanisms, internal runtime behavior, packaging, resource resolution,
trust mechanisms, compatibility resolution, and other realization
details to separate specifications or proprietary implementations.

# 🌐 From Platform Architecture to Ecosystem Participation

Governed execution infrastructure may support more than centrally
operated software services.

The research explores a federated model in which independently operated
**Deployment Units** may serve as:

-   technical boundaries
-   governance boundaries
-   operational boundaries
-   trust boundaries
-   data-sovereignty boundaries
-   economic participation boundaries

Within this model, ecosystem participants may:

-   build governed capabilities and extensions
-   operate execution infrastructure
-   integrate execution platforms with other systems
-   contribute domain knowledge
-   create adoption
-   educate users and communities
-   serve local, regional, or industry-specific markets
-   and participate economically in the value they help create

CaralisLabs may provide common architecture, governance mechanisms,
standards, reference implementations, deployment lifecycle technologies,
research, and continued platform development without being required to
centrally operate every execution environment.

The foundational principle is:

> **Value should follow attributable contribution.**

Execution evidence may help make contribution more visible. However,
attribution does not automatically create economic entitlement. Economic
participation requires explicitly defined programs, agreements,
licenses, or other commercial mechanisms.

# 🌍 Looking Forward

The long-term vision explored throughout this repository is a
progression:

> From governing systems

toward

> governing execution

The long-term vision also extends:

> From centrally delivered platforms

toward

> federated execution ecosystems

and:

> From ecosystem contribution

toward

> attributable and sustainable economic participation

As AI systems become increasingly autonomous, execution itself becomes
an architectural concern deserving dedicated models, platforms,
governance mechanisms, and economic frameworks.

As execution infrastructure becomes independently deployable and
operable, Deployment Units may enable local businesses, specialized
services, capability ecosystems, and new forms of participation in the
value created through governed execution.
