# Abstract

This publication argues that computing is evolving toward an Execution
Economy, where governed participants exchange execution as a first-class
economic activity. While Artificial Intelligence reduces the cost of
generating intelligence, sustainable economic value emerges only when
intelligence is translated into trusted, governed, accountable
execution.

This thesis positions the existing TextFind and PER architecture,
together with the RFC portfolio, as an exploration of the infrastructure
primitives required to support such an economy.

# Chapter 1 --- Introduction

Throughout history, economic systems expanded by increasing the scale at
which assets could be exchanged.

Roads enabled physical goods exchange.

Telecommunications enabled information exchange.

The Internet enabled global digital exchange.

Artificial Intelligence enables the large-scale production of
intelligence.

Yet intelligence itself is not the final asset.

Organizations derive value from execution.

The central claim of this thesis is:

> The next major economic abstraction is governed execution.

# Chapter 3 --- Why AI Is Not the Destination

The current narrative assumes increasingly capable AI systems will solve
most enterprise challenges.

However:

AI solves intelligence generation.

It does not inherently solve:

-   Governance
-   Trust
-   Authority
-   Accountability
-   Provenance

These concerns become more important as AI capabilities increase.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Chapter 4 --- Intelligence Versus Execution

Value Creation Chain:

Knowledge ↓ Intelligence ↓ Decision ↓ Action ↓ Execution ↓ Outcome

Organizations purchase outcomes.

Execution is the mechanism that transforms intelligence into outcomes.

# Chapter 5 --- The Limits of Context Engineering

Context Engineering is an important advancement.

It improves:

-   Memory
-   Relevance
-   Retrieval
-   Personalization

However, context alone cannot answer:

-   Should this action be allowed?
-   Who authorized it?
-   Can it be delegated?
-   Can the outcome be trusted?

Context improves intelligence.

Governance improves execution.
