# Building a Real Product in the Age of AI: What It Actually Takes

*The journey of a sole founder, from TextFind to CARALIS OS, and the work that happens beyond writing code.*

AI has changed what an individual can accomplish.

Today, a single person can explore ideas, develop software, investigate technical problems, create content, and perform work that previously required teams with different specializations.

But does that mean building a real technology product has become easy?

After nearly four decades working with software, and several years developing my own product as a sole founder, my experience suggests something more interesting.

**AI has made ambitious product development more achievable for individuals. But building a real product still requires far more than generating software.**

And much of that work remains invisible.

## It Started With TextFind

Around 2022, I began developing TextFind, a platform intended to help people discover, process, and work with information.

By 2023, I was publicly demonstrating capabilities such as semantic search, question answering, speech-to-text processing, and working with information from different sources.

Those demonstrations represented real progress, but they were also the beginning of a much longer journey.

As development continued, I encountered questions that went beyond the original application.

How could existing capabilities become more reusable? How could they be combined in different ways? How could the underlying technology support applications and use cases that hadn't been anticipated when it was first designed?

Answering those questions required more than adding features.

I had to revisit earlier decisions, improve existing components, re-architect parts of the system, and gradually generalize capabilities that had originally been developed for specific purposes.

Some changes were incremental. Others required substantial work.

There were times when the most important progress wasn't a new feature but a better foundation that would make future development possible.

**Looking back, getting to where we are today would not have been possible without building TextFind first.**

The experience gained from developing and improving it became the foundation for what followed.

## From an Application to a Broader Vision

Over time, the work evolved into something larger than information discovery and processing.

I began thinking about knowledge differently.

What if knowledge could become more than something people search for, read, or consume?

What if individuals and organizations could transform their expertise into reusable capabilities that perform useful work?

And what if those who contribute such capabilities could participate in the economic value generated when others use them?

These questions led toward the idea of **executable knowledge** and, eventually, the broader vision we call the **Execution Economy**.

Our mission at CaralisLabs is to make executable knowledge accessible and economically valuable, enabling individuals and organizations to contribute capabilities, combine them with others, and participate in the value their contributions create.

CARALIS OS is the product we're building to help make that vision possible.

But another fundamental question emerged along the way.

If capabilities can perform work, who controls their execution?

## Why Governed Execution Matters

As AI becomes increasingly capable of proposing decisions and actions, the distinction between intelligence and execution becomes more important.

Intelligence can help determine what might be done.

But performing work on behalf of individuals and organizations introduces responsibilities that cannot simply be delegated to an AI model.

Who is authorized to execute a capability? Under what conditions? What policies apply? How can the results be verified? Who remains accountable?

These questions shaped one of the fundamental principles behind CARALIS OS.

**Governed execution must be part of the foundation, not an afterthought.**

We are building an environment where capabilities can be executed within defined controls, with authorization, traceability, and appropriate oversight.

This is important not only for technical reliability but also for the economic model we're pursuing.

If knowledge and capabilities are to be contributed by different people and organizations, trust and accountability become essential.

## Building the Foundations of CARALIS OS

As the vision expanded, the underlying technology had to evolve with it.

This wasn't a matter of discarding TextFind and starting again.

It was a process of building upon what already existed, identifying which capabilities could be generalized, improving their implementation, and developing the foundations needed for a broader product.

Several important concepts emerged.

**Runtimes** provide the environments where capabilities and services perform their work.

**Platforms** bring capabilities and services together so that people and organizations can access them and put them to use.

**Deployment Units** provide the operational foundation for deploying, managing, and operating these environments within defined boundaries.

These concepts are relatively easy to describe.

Making them work together reliably is another matter.

Each stage of development introduces new requirements, dependencies, and decisions. Improvements in one area can reveal limitations elsewhere.

This is where years of accumulated engineering work become important.

The product we are preparing today exists because of the work that preceded it: the original implementation, the experiments, the improvements, the redesigns, and the continuous generalization of capabilities.

## A Product Must Be Repeatable

One of the most significant differences between demonstrating software and delivering a product is operational repeatability.

A demonstration may work perfectly in the environment where it was developed.

But bringing a product to users requires something more.

It must be possible to establish environments, deploy components, configure dependencies, manage resources, and operate the system consistently.

For CARALIS OS, repeatable deployment has become an important part of the development effort.

We need to be able to bring Deployment Units into operation without manually reconstructing the entire environment each time.

This requires engineering work that users may never see directly.

Yet it is fundamental to making the product usable.

**A product isn't ready simply because its individual components work. Those components must work together, and the result must be reproducible.**

AI can accelerate parts of this work, but integration, operational consistency, and responsibility for the outcome remain essential.

## The Work Beyond Engineering

There is another side to building a product that receives much less attention.

As a sole founder, I don't have separate departments responsible for research, strategy, marketing, communication, or business development.

Those responsibilities exist regardless of whether the company has one employee or hundreds.

Over time, several activities have become part of my regular working routine, often every week.

**Market intelligence** means following technological developments, employment trends, emerging business models, and changes in how organizations operate.

**Strategic validation** means continuously questioning assumptions and comparing our direction with what is happening in the market.

**Positioning** means learning how to explain what we're building, why it matters, and how it differs from existing approaches.

**Communication** means writing articles, participating in professional discussions, publishing on social media, and experimenting with new ways to make complex ideas accessible.

**Market awareness and relationships** mean understanding other organizations, identifying potential collaborators, engaging with professional communities, and preparing to connect with future users and partners.

These activities don't begin when engineering ends.

They happen alongside development.

And they matter because a technically capable product still needs a purpose that people understand and a market in which it can create value.

## Learning Without Chasing Every Trend

The past few years have been extraordinary for the software industry.

We've watched successive waves of excitement around generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation, agents, multi-agent systems, and increasingly autonomous software.

Each development deserves attention.

But following the industry doesn't mean rebuilding a product around every announcement.

As an independent founder, I have to be particularly careful about where I invest time and resources.

We have continued studying these developments, evaluating their implications, and questioning our own assumptions.

At the same time, we've tried to preserve a consistent direction.

The objective has never been to predict which technology company or framework will dominate the market.

It has been to identify problems worth solving regardless of which technologies become fashionable.

That discipline has helped maintain continuity throughout the development journey.

## What AI Has Changed for Me

Perhaps the most remarkable part of this experience is how AI has changed the practical possibilities of working independently.

I bring decades of software experience, but there are limits to what any individual can know or accomplish alone.

AI has helped extend those limits.

It assists with engineering, investigation, implementation, testing, learning, and exploring alternatives.

It also helps with market research, strategic analysis, writing, and communication.

Recently, I've been experimenting with AI-generated interviews and videos to communicate ideas about CARALIS OS and the Execution Economy.

Activities that would once have required several specialists are becoming accessible to an individual.

But there's an important distinction.

**AI expands what I can accomplish. It doesn't replace the responsibility for deciding what is worth doing.**

I still have to evaluate its suggestions, understand the consequences of technical decisions, establish priorities, and bring the work together into something coherent.

And sometimes the most valuable use of AI isn't generating an answer.

It's helping me examine an assumption, understand an unfamiliar subject, or consider an alternative I might otherwise have overlooked.

In that sense, AI has become useful across nearly every aspect of the founder's work.

## Approaching the Next Chapter

Today, we're approaching an important milestone in the development of CARALIS OS.

The foundations developed over several years are coming together as we prepare an operational Deployment Unit capable of bringing Platforms, Runtimes, services, and executable capabilities into a working environment.

This is a significant step toward making the product available to users.

It also represents something more personal.

What began with TextFind has evolved through years of development, experimentation, improvement, and architectural evolution into a much broader undertaking.

Along the way, I've had to learn not only how to build the technology but also how to understand the market, communicate the mission, establish priorities, and prepare for the next stage.

AI has made much of this work more achievable for an individual.

But the experience has reinforced something I've learned throughout my career.

**Creating a real product is not simply about writing software. It's about bringing technology, operations, purpose, strategy, and people together.**

The mission remains clear.

We want to make executable knowledge accessible and enable more people to participate in the economic value their knowledge creates.

And after years of building the foundations, we're getting ready to bring that vision into practical use.

The journey continues.
