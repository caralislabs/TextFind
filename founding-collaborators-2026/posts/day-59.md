For years, we've been asking whether AI will replace our jobs.

But perhaps we're asking the wrong question.

AI is changing how organizations allocate capital, organize work, and create value.

And this transformation is no longer limited to the software industry.

Organizations are investing heavily in AI. But buying technology is one thing. Redesigning workflows, governing automated execution, and making those investments productive is another.

Many organizations will struggle with that transition.

And what about the people?

We've traditionally responded to industry disruptions by learning new skills, changing employers, or starting businesses.

But what happens when the disruption reaches nearly every industry?

**Where do we go when everyone is facing the same transformation?**

Perhaps the answer isn't simply to find another place in the existing economy.

Perhaps it's time to rethink how we participate in creating value.

What if our knowledge, expertise, technology, infrastructure, and ideas could become contributions to solutions that others can build upon?

What if those contributions could continue generating value beyond the work originally performed?

**That's one of the questions behind the Execution Economy we're developing at CaralisLabs.**

We're not suggesting that everyone needs to become an AI researcher, software developer, or entrepreneur.

We're exploring how people with different capabilities and resources could participate in something larger.

**The future of work shouldn't be reduced to competing for fewer positions. It should also be about creating new ways to participate in the value technology generates.**
